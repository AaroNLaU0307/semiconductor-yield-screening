# SPC report

Generated by `scripts/run_pipeline.py`. Control limits are estimated on the **train** partition and applied unchanged to holdout. This page contains the first of the two declared holdout reads (DECISIONS.md D12).

## 1. Headline

**The pre-declared out of control flag is degenerate and carries no information.** It flags 468 of 471 holdout records, leaving only 3 in control, so the contingency test has almost nothing to compare against and its risk ratio is not estimable (Fisher exact p = 1.0000).

This is a flaw in the flag definition frozen at Gate 1, not a property of the process, and the cause is arithmetic rather than mysterious. DECISIONS.md D8 defined a record as out of control if **any** applied rule fires on **any** charted sensor. The measured per sensor flag rates in section 2 run from 3 percent to 60 percent, and a union over ten such sensors reaches roughly 99 percent by construction. A flag that fires on almost every record cannot discriminate anything.

The declared result is reported below exactly as it was specified, rather than replaced. Section 5 adds a severity sweep as a post-hoc diagnostic, reported in full so that no single threshold is selected after the fact.

**The sweep finds nothing either.** Across all 12 testable threshold and rule set combinations, the smallest BH adjusted p is 1.0000, and no combination shows an association between out of control flags and holdout failure. The plain reading is that on this data these control charts do not anticipate failures on held out records. With 26 holdout failures the sweep has limited power, so this is weak evidence of no effect rather than evidence of no effect, and it is reported because it is what was found.

## 3. Method

I-MR charts on individuals, with the moving range of consecutive records.

| property | value |
| --- | --- |
| centre | mean of train individuals |
| sigma estimate | MR-bar / 1.128 |
| individuals limits | centre +/- 2.66 * MR-bar |
| moving range UCL | 3.267 * MR-bar |
| limits estimated on | train only |
| run rule scope | within a partition, never spanning the boundary |
| run rule flag point | the point at which the pattern completes |

Applied Nelson rules:

| rule | condition |
| --- | --- |
| Nelson 1 | one point beyond 3 sigma |
| Nelson 2 | nine consecutive points on the same side of centre |
| Nelson 3 | six consecutive points steadily increasing or decreasing |
| Nelson 5 | two of three consecutive points beyond 2 sigma, same side |
| Nelson 6 | four of five consecutive points beyond 1 sigma, same side |

Rules 4, 7 and 8 are excluded. They assume a stable within subgroup sigma and roughly continuous readings, and this data has median imputed values creating exact ties, a 37 minute median cadence with gaps up to 48.6 hours, and no rational subgroups. Under those conditions they fire on artefacts of the data preparation rather than on process behaviour.


**Stated limitation.** I-MR assumes approximately normal and independent individuals. Neither holds cleanly here. These limits are a screening heuristic for where to look, not calibrated false alarm rates. The contingency test below is the check that does not depend on the limits being calibrated: it asks only whether flagged records fail more often.

## 6. Process drift, a separate observation

The holdout out of control rate in section 2 is higher than the train rate for 9 of the 10 charted sensors, 5 of them by a factor of two or more; the exception is sensor_103 (0.3878 on train, 0.3715 on holdout). The limits are estimated on train and applied unchanged, so this says the holdout period genuinely sits further from the train centre lines than the train period did. That is consistent with the failure rate non-stationarity recorded in DECISIONS.md D0, and for a process engineer it is the more useful signal here than the contingency test: the process moved.


## 7. Reading this correctly

An out of control signal says a sensor moved relative to its own train behaviour. It does not say that movement caused a failure, and it does not say the sensor is controllable. The contingency test measures whether the two coincide on held out records, which is the strongest claim this design supports.
